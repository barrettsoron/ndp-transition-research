## Six seats will be up for grabs within the next six months, with all four parties having ridings to defend

Now that Monday's three byelections are in the books, it's time to look ahead to the next set of byelections — and what Monday's results might tell us about what's in store.

There are currently five vacancies in the House of Commons and a sixth is slated to occur in the coming weeks. Those vacancies are:

- **Saint-Hyacinthe—Bagot—Acton:** This Quebec riding is located between Montreal and Quebec and is the centre of the province's dairy industry. Bloc MP Simon-Pierre Savard-Tremblay vacated the seat in June and is running for the Parti Québécois in the ongoing provincial election.
- **Scarborough North:** Liberal MP Shaun Chen vacated this Toronto seat a few weeks ago due to health issues caused by a car accident last year that makes it difficult for him to travel to Ottawa.
- **Rosemont—La Petite-Patrie:** Alexandre Boulerice left the NDP caucus earlier this year to sit as an Independent after announcing his plans to run for Québec Solidaire in the provincial election. Boulerice was the last surviving NDP Quebec MP elected in the Orange Wave of 2011.
- **Laurier—Sainte-Marie:** Located next door to Rosemont—La Petite-Patrie in downtown Montreal, Liberal MP Steven Guilbeault first left the Liberal candidate and then resigned this seat, citing disagreements with the Carney government's environmental policies.
- **Yorkton—Melville:** Conservative MP Cathay Wagantall resigned this rural Saskatchewan seat on Monday. She was first elected in 2015.
- **Brantford—Brant South:** Conservative MP Larry Brock has signalled his intention to resign this seat in southwestern Ontario later this month. (Note: the riding is officially called Brantford—Brant South—Six Nations, but will have the "Six Nations" portion dropped from the name in a few weeks at the request of the Six Nations of the Grand River.)

As Saint-Hyacinthe—Bagot—Acton was vacated in June, Mark Carney has until December 19 to call this byelection. Assuming he does not want to call a holiday byelection campaign, it seems more likely this byelection will be called sometime in October or November, after the completion of the Quebec election. If the Liberals intend to call all six byelections at the same time and avoid too much overlap with municipal elections (the last of which will take place in Saskatchewan on November 9), the calls could come in early November, with the byelections being held in early December.

(If the Liberals decide not to call these byelections at the same time, then we could see five of the six called in early 2027.)

This is an interesting set of byelections, with three in Quebec, two in Ontario and one in Saskatchewan. There is a mix of urban seats (Rosemont—La Petite-Patrie, Laurier—Sainte-Marie and Scarborough North), seats with a rural component but centred on a small city (Saint-Hyacinthe—Bagot—Acton and Brantford—Brant South) and a rural riding (Yorkton—Melville). In the last election, two were won by the Liberals, two by the Conservatives, one by the NDP and one by the Bloc. So, every party will be playing defence.

And at least one party will be playing offence.

The results from the last election suggest that a few ridings are unlikely to change hands. The Conservatives won **Yorkton—Melville** with 77.6% of the vote, beating the Liberals by more than 60 points. The Liberals won **Scarborough North** with 63%, beating the Conservatives by nearly 30 points. And **Laurier—Sainte-Marie** went to the Liberals by a margin of 33 points over the NDP. Barring an upset, the incumbents should easily win these three seats and they are all projected as '**Safe**' for the incumbent parties.

There will be things to watch in these three safe ridings beyond who wins them — the Liberals will want to hold their vote in their two seats and see how much progress they can make in Yorkton—Melville, the NDP (and Bloc) would like to put up some good numbers in Laurier—Sainte-Marie and the Conservatives desperately need to halt the hemorrhage of support they've suffered in the last six byelections — but the real interest will be in the other three seats.

The results in Chicoutimi—Le Fjord on Monday have to be making the Liberals kick themselves for not calling a byelection in **Saint-Hyacinthe—Bagot—Acton** at the same time (which they could have, as it has been vacant for longer than both Chicoutimi—Le Fjord and Beaches—East York). If the Liberals can get over 50% of the vote in a riding like Chicoutimi—Le Fjord, they can get that share of the vote in almost any riding in Quebec. The Bloc won Saint-Hyacinthe—Bagot—Acton by just over 10 points, but the swing that took place in Chicoutimi—Le Fjord was big enough for the Liberals to easily overcome that margin if repeated here.

Considering what just happened on Monday, the Bloc will have to work harder than it might have thought to hold this seat. Nevertheless, with the polls where they are in Quebec at the moment, my projections classify it as a **Bloc Toss**.

**Rosemont—La Petite-Patrie** will be a key test for the New Democrats. Since 2011, this riding increasingly became a Boulerice seat rather than an NDP seat, but the New Democrats will nevertheless be the incumbents (and have a decent candidate in Pierre Céré). The party demonstrated in Beaches—East York that, when they put some resources into a byelection, they can put up a strong result. Quebec, however, is a different beast for the NDP and this byelection will be a challenge for them. Flipping it will also be a challenge for the Liberals as the last six byelections have not shown big boosts for the Liberals in urban ridings like this one, and their focus might be elsewhere. The model has it as an **NDP Toss**, but how much of Boulerice's coattails the NDP can still ride is hard to project.

The Liberals might instead have their focus concentrated on **Brantford—Brant South**. The Conservatives won it by just over 11 points, but the shift in the polls we've seen in Ontario against the Conservatives has put this riding in doubt. The model has it as a **Liberal Toss**, and chatter in Conservative circles is also that the party could struggle to hold this seat.

That would be a tremendous blow for Pierre Poilievre. The Conservatives have held this part of Ontario since the 2008 election. It's never been a rock-solid seat, as the Conservative vote ranged between 40% and 41% in the 2015, 2019 and 2021 elections. But it is nevertheless a seat the Conservatives have held for nearly 20 years. If the Conservatives can't hold Brantford—Brant South, there are many Conservative-held ridings in southwestern, central and eastern Ontario with a similar profile to Brantford—Brant South that will suddenly look in real danger for the first time since the Canadian Alliance and Progressive Conservatives merged to form the Conservative Party in 2003.

The stakes will be high in these six byelections. If we're still in a similar public opinion environment as today when they occur, the Liberals will enter the byelections the same way that they did the three on Monday: confident they will hold what they've got, and confident that they can make some gains. The Conservatives will be under tremendous pressure not to lose another seat and to avoid serious vote-share losses, while the Bloc will have to make good on its claim that they're the only viable alternative to the Liberals in Quebec. Avi Lewis will have to prove that the New Democrats aren't entirely doomed in the province and, in order to avoid the mixed reviews from Monday, show their vote has recovered at least somewhat in ridings like Yorkton—Melville and Brantford—Brant South, where they could expect double-digit support as recently as the 2021 election.

Let the next round of byelections begin!
