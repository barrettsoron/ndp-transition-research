Wednesday September 2, 2026 | OTTAWA, ON

**The federal government has extended the fuel excise tax** for another five months, to January 31, 2027.

Minister of Finance and National Revenue François-Philippe Champagne delivered the announcement today inside a Canadian Tire store in Ottawa.

He announced temporary suspension extension to help "bring down everyday costs for Canadians, including truckers and businesses in the food, agriculture, housing, construction, and delivery sectors".

## Extending 50% to March 31, 2027

As well, the federal government will apply 50% of the regular excise tax rate from February 1 through March 31, 2027.

The pre-announced reduction for February and March 2027 is an indication that the tax suspension will be fully phased out starting with the April 1, 2027 start to the 2026-2027 fiscal year.

## A continued response

The federal fuel excise tax was first temporarily suspended on April 20, 2026.

Back in April, Carney temporarily removed the 10-cent federal excise tax on a litre of gas and four cents per litre on diesel until September 7 (Labour Day).

The Ministry of Finance says that has saved Canadian consumers 10 cents per litre on gasoline and unleaded aviation gasoline, 11 cents per litre on leaded aviation gasoline, and 4 cents per litre on diesel and aviation fuel.

As Canada continues to build a stronger, more resilient, and more independent economy, the government is taking practical steps to reduce costs today while investing in long-term prosperity. Extending fuel tax relief is one of the measures helping families, workers, and businesses navigate global economic uncertainty.

The government says it is also taking broader action to make life more affordable, including through the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit, although the amount received every quarter by about 22 million low-income Canadians is lower than it was when there was a consumer carbon tax rebate.

## Overall impact

While a price break is always helpful, the broader context is that the cost of living challenges for Canadians is far broader and much deeper than the price of gasoline-at-the-pump.

As well, a tax break means less revenues for the government as they continue to try and cover a range of obligations to the budget and to Canadians.

## Conservative response

The Conservatives have been calling for a continued hold on the fuel excise tax. The Liberals have responded in agreement.

Today Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre says the next fuel excise tax extension should extend further, to Canada Day 2027. He describes today's extension as "another Liberal half measure".

## NDP response

The federal NDP opposes the excise tax measure. NDP Leader Avi Lewis describes it as "corporate welfare" that rewards oil giants who are already profiting from "Trump's illegal war in Iran".

The tax comes off what consumers pay at the pump (though not a big help), not what taxes oil companies pay to the government, but the underlying structure of maintaining oil company stability is in fact maintained by a retail-level extension.

> "It's time for a windfall tax on the big oil giants that are set to make $100 billion in profits this year," said Lewis in a statement. "Doing so will generate billions in new revenue that we can use to give Canadians real relief at gas stations and grocery checkouts."
